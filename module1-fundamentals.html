Module 1 · 20 minutes

How does a company decide the price?

Every price on earth is set one of three ways. Learn the three levers, play with them, and you’ll never look at a price tag the same way again.

The big idea

Price sits between a floor and a ceiling

🧱 The floor

Your cost. Sell below this and every sale loses money. This is set by you.

🏁 The middle

Competitors. Customers compare. Price far from rivals and you’d better have a reason.

☁️ The ceiling

Customer value. The most someone will pay. Set by them, not you.

🎤 Trainer says “A price is never a random number. It lives in a band — above your cost, below what the customer thinks it’s worth. The three strategies are just three different ways to pick a spot inside that band.”

The three pricing strategies

Strategy 1

Cost-Plus

Take your cost, add a fixed markup. Simple and safe.

Used by: retailers, contractors, restaurants, most manufacturers.

Risk: ignores what the customer would happily pay — you may leave money on the table.

Strategy 2

Competitive

Price around what rivals charge — match, undercut, or sit just above.

Used by: petrol pumps, airlines, e-commerce, telecom.

Risk: a race to the bottom — everyone’s margin bleeds.

Strategy 3

Value-Based

Price on the value the customer perceives — not your cost.

Used by: Apple, luxury brands, premium SaaS, consultants.

Risk: hardest to pull off — you must genuinely deliver (or signal) more value.

Pricing in the wild · real brands you know

Spot the strategy in everyday India

Cost-Plus
  • Amul — pays farmers, adds a modest margin, prints an MRP. Deliberately affordable.
  • DMart — buys cheap in bulk, adds a thin fixed markup, wins on volume.
  • Your kirana / a contractor — “cost + my cut.”
Competitive
  • Petrol pumps — prices move almost in lockstep, revised daily.
  • Amazon vs Flipkart — algorithms re-match each other’s prices during sales.
  • Jio, 2016 — crashed data prices and forced Airtel & Vodafone to follow — a price war that reshaped an industry.
Value-Based
  • Apple iPhone — priced on brand and experience, not on what the parts cost.
  • Starbucks — a ₹350 latte next to a ₹20 cutting chai. You’re buying the couch and the wifi.
  • IPL / concert tickets — priced on the thrill, not the seat’s “cost.”
Bonus · Dynamic pricing

A fourth flavour you’ll meet in the simulation: the price moves with demand. Uber/Ola surge in the rain, airline seats that cost more as the flight fills, Swiggy/Zomato fees that rise at dinner-time, hotels on a festival weekend. Same seat, same room — different price by the minute.

🎤 Trainer says Play a 60-second game: name a brand, class shouts the strategy. Maggi? (competitive/value). Rolls-Royce? (value). Indian Railways general class? (cost-plus/social). It gets them seeing pricing everywhere.
