Raise the price. Do you make more money — or less?

The answer is “it depends,” and by the end of this module you’ll be able to find the answer instead of guessing. This is the thinking you’ll use to win the simulation.

Law #1

Higher price → fewer buyers

Almost always true. The real question is how many fewer. A ₹10 rise on chewing gum loses tons of buyers; a ₹10 lakh rise on a Ferrari loses almost none. That responsiveness has a name: price elasticity.

Elastic

Stretchy demand

Small price change → big drop in buyers. Think: soft drinks, one brand of rice, budget phones. Here, discounts can pay for themselves and high prices are dangerous.

Inelastic

Stubborn demand

Big price change → few buyers leave. Think: medicine, petrol, salt, the only cable in your city. Here, raising price grows profit. This is pricing power.

Elasticity in the real world

Why the government taxes petrol, not vegetables

🪨 Inelastic = pricing power

  • Petrol & cigarettes — heavily taxed precisely because people keep buying. Demand barely flinches.
  • Life-saving medicine — why India caps prices; sellers otherwise have huge power.
  • Netflix / Amazon Prime — hiked prices repeatedly; most of us grumbled and stayed.
  • Salt (the Dandi lesson) — a tiny necessity nobody stops buying.

🎈 Elastic = handle with care

  • Movie tickets — cheap Tuesdays exist because weekday demand is very price-sensitive.
  • Soft drinks & snacks — switch brands in a heartbeat for ₹5.
  • Budget smartphones — a ₹1,000 gap sends buyers to a rival.
  • Air travel (economy) — fares swing wildly to chase price-sensitive flyers.
🎤 Trainer says “Here’s the CEO’s secret question about any product: ‘If I raise the price 10%, do my customers leave?’ If not, you’re sitting on pricing power and probably charging too little.”

The mind games

₹499 or ₹500? The 1-rupee trick that isn’t about 1 rupee

Charm pricing

₹499 reads as “four-hundred-something,” not five hundred. The left digit anchors us. Used everywhere in retail.

Anchoring

Show a ₹9,999 plan first and the ₹4,999 plan feels cheap. The first number sets the frame.

The decoy

Popcorn: Small ₹150, Large ₹400. Add a “Medium ₹380” nobody buys — and Large suddenly feels smart.

Seen in the wild: Big Bazaar’s endless ₹99 / ₹199 tags · app subscriptions at ₹499 not ₹500 · PVR combos that make the large tub look like a bargain · D-Mart’s “everyday low” signalling.

🎤 Trainer says Ask the room: “Who has an app subscription that’s ₹X99? Who noticed at the time?” Everyone raises a hand. That’s psychology beating math.
